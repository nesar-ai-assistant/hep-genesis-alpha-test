# How many arXiv papers on galaxy cluster physics appeared in 2025? A model-based estimate

## Abstract

No source located in this investigation gives a direct count of 2025 arXiv papers on galaxy cluster physics. The estimate is therefore built as a sourced total multiplied by assumed fractions.

The total is sourced. The year-in-review paper "Astrophysics Wrapped 2025" (arXiv:2602.12303) reports 18,660 astro-ph papers in 2025, counting primary submissions only, against 16,333 in 2024. It also gives counts for each subcategory.

The fractions are not sourced. Every within-category share of cluster papers used below is an assumption. No bibliometric source and no figure read could replace them.

On that basis, the reconciled estimates for 2025 are:

- **Strict count** (clusters as the main subject): about 920, range roughly 660 to 1,250.
- **Broad count** (clusters as an important tool or sample): about 1,640, range roughly 1,290 to 2,020, plus an unquantified number of cross-lists from gr-qc and hep-ph.

The uncertainty comes almost entirely from the assumed fractions, not from the total. The critic contested all three findings from this run, mainly because the central fractions are not anchored to data.

## Background

**The total count.** The key source is arXiv:2602.12303, "Astrophysics Wrapped 2025: Year-in-Review of Every Astrophysics arXiv Paper from 2025" (Lewis, Shah, Alfred). It compiles statistics on every 2025 astro-ph paper: keywords, subfields, telescopes, journals and geography.

Before this run, the total rested on an indirect lead. A web-search snippet, not read at the source, said the Open Journal of Astrophysics published 213 papers in 2025, "only just over 1%" of astro-ph submissions. That implies roughly 17,000 to 20,000 new astro-ph submissions, or about 20,000 to 25,000 including cross-lists.

**A second dataset.** AstroMLab 5 (arXiv:2511.12353) provides structured summaries and extracted concepts for 408,590 astro-ph papers from 1992 through July 2025. This fits a recent rate of roughly 18,000 to 20,000 or more papers per year. Its concept tags were not accessed in this run.

**The fraction.** No paper found in the record reports what share of astro-ph is about galaxy clusters. The earlier working brief assumed, from general knowledge, a share of about 5 to 8%. That gave a strict central value near 1,200 and a broad range of 2,000 to 3,000.

**Earlier runs.** Two earlier runs on this thread produced conflicting strict estimates of about 1,200 and about 640. They differed mainly in the fractions assumed for astro-ph.CO and astro-ph.GA:

| Earlier estimate | CO share | GA share |
|---|---|---|
| About 1,200 | 15–25% | 8–12% |
| About 640 | 12% | 4% |

## Results

### The 2025 base count from arXiv:2602.12303

The full text of arXiv:2602.12303 was downloaded and read. It states that 18,660 research papers were published on arXiv under Astrophysics in 2025, compared with 16,333 in 2024. That is an average of 1,555 papers per month and year-on-year growth of 18,660/16,333 = 1.142, or +14.2%.

The board treats this base as primary submissions only, with no cross-lists. The open question of whether cross-lists are excluded has not been fully settled against arXiv's own statistics.

The subcategory counts used below are:

| Subcategory | 2025 papers |
|---|---|
| astro-ph.CO | 2,789 |
| astro-ph.GA | 4,761 |
| astro-ph.HE | 3,869 |
| astro-ph.IM | 1,722 |
| general astro-ph | 268 |

The 18,660 figure falls inside the 17,000 to 20,000 range inferred from the OJAp lead. That agreement supports the base.

### How the year-in-review assigns keywords and sub-fields

The methods text of arXiv:2602.12303, cited verbatim, explains the assignment:

- **Keywords.** 12,753 of the 18,660 papers (68%) carry author keywords. Each remaining paper is given three self-assigned keywords, chosen by matching common words in its title and abstract against a list of about 1,000 frequent author keywords.
- **Sub-fields.** These are assigned one per paper, from a list of about 100 terms.

This has a practical consequence for any future figure read:

- A sub-field bar count in Fig. 13, divided by 18,660, would be a paper share directly.
- A keyword count in Fig. 12 cannot be converted that way, because papers carry a variable number of keywords (three or more).

### Qualitative anchors from the paper's tables

Two tables give only qualitative information.

- **Table 8**, the top 25 author keywords (excluding self-assigned ones), contains no galaxy-cluster entry. It does include Cosmology, Dark Matter, Surveys, Hydrodynamics, Data Analysis and Interstellar Medium.
- **Table 7** lists "Galaxy Clusters" as the top keyword of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, with 7,837 author-weighted contributions.

These anchors constrain the share only weakly. The critic raised two problems:

- Cluster terms may be split across variants ("galaxy clusters", "clusters of galaxies", "intracluster medium"). That would keep any single variant out of the top 25 even if the combined share is substantial.
- The value of 7,837 for one keyword is large relative to an 18,660-paper base. Its definition should be checked before it is relied on even qualitatively.

### Reconciled 2025 estimate

All fractions below are assumptions applied to the sourced subcategory counts. astro-ph.SR and astro-ph.EP are set to zero, to exclude star, globular and open clusters.

**Strict estimate (clusters as the main subject):**

| Category | Assumed share | Papers | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO | 15% | 2,789 | 418 |
| GA | 6% | 4,761 | 286 |
| HE | 4% | 3,869 | 155 |
| IM | 3% | 1,722 | 52 |
| general astro-ph | 3% | 268 | 8 |
| **Total** | | | **about 920** |

- Low end, with shares of 12/4/2.5/2/2%: 661.
- High end, with shares of 20/8/6/4/4%: 1,251.

**Broad estimate (clusters as an important tool or sample):**

| Category | Assumed share | Papers | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO | 25% | 2,789 | 697 |
| GA | 12% | 4,761 | 571 |
| HE | 7% | 3,869 | 271 |
| IM | 5% | 1,722 | 86 |
| general astro-ph | 5% | 268 | 13 |
| **Total** | | | **about 1,640** |

- Low end, with shares of 20/10/5/3/3%: about 1,290.
- High end, with shares of 30/15/9/6/6%: about 2,020.
- Cross-lists from gr-qc and hep-ph would add to this total but could not be quantified.

**Reconciling the earlier runs.** The about-1,200 estimate sits near the top of the strict range, and the about-640 estimate sits near the bottom. Two pieces of evidence were used to move the central value:

- The absence of "Galaxy Clusters" from Table 8 was taken to argue against GA shares of 8–12%.
- Its appearance as a top institutional keyword was taken to argue against a negligible share.

As noted above, the critic judged the first inference unvalidated. The chosen central fractions are therefore effectively midpoints, not data-constrained values.

**2024 back-projection.** Assuming a constant share and dividing by 1.142 gives about 800 to 805 strict papers and about 1,430 broad papers for 2024. This is a pure rescaling of the 2025 numbers. It is not an independent check and should not be read as evidence of robustness.

## Discussion

### What the run established

The main gain is a sourced and internally consistent base. It is 18,660 primary astro-ph papers in 2025 with a per-category breakdown, and it agrees with the indirect OJAp-based estimate of 17,000 to 20,000. With the base fixed, the final number depends almost entirely on how much of CO, GA and HE is assumed to be cluster work.

### Comparison with the earlier brief

The reconciled strict value of about 920 is lower than the earlier brief's central value of about 1,200 but overlaps its 1,000 to 1,500 range. The reconciled broad range of 1,290 to 2,020 is lower than the brief's 2,000 to 3,000 and touches it only at the top end.

The shift comes from smaller assumed fractions, not from new data. Both sets of numbers should be read as plausible orders of magnitude, about 10³ strict and about 1.5–2 × 10³ broad, not as measurements.

### Dead ends

A search for a citable bibliometric or topic-modelling study reporting a galaxy-cluster share of astro-ph found nothing usable. It consisted of three arXiv abstract searches (topic modelling of astro-ph, bibliometrics of subfields, LLM knowledge graphs of astronomy) and one web search.

- The closest lead was an LDA lead-lag study covering 1992–2011. It is too old and was found without an arXiv ID.
- arXiv:2406.01391, a knowledge graph on interdisciplinarity, gives no subfield shares in its abstract.

The planned figure read of Figs. 12 and 13 failed because no tool could read image pixels.

### Cautions raised by the critic during planning

- A "Gravitational Lensing" bar, if present, should not be counted toward the strict share. Most lensing papers are not about clusters, so it could serve at most as an upper bound for the broad count.
- Any keyword-frequency value would need the mean number of keywords per paper before it could replace an assumed fraction.

## Limitations

**The fractions are unmeasured.** Every within-category cluster share is an assumption. A factor-of-two difference in the CO and GA shares, which is roughly the spread between the two earlier runs, drives most of the uncertainty.

**No direct measurement was possible.** There was no ADS or arXiv API access for a keyword-facet count. The recommended query is abs:("galaxy cluster" OR "clusters of galaxies" OR intracluster) year:2025 collection:astronomy, with star clusters excluded. It was not run, and no hand sample was taken to measure the false-positive rate.

**The best figures went unread.** Figs. 12 and 13 of arXiv:2602.12303 were extracted but could not be read, so whether a cluster sub-field bar exists is unknown.

**Cross-lists are unquantified.** The cross-list contribution from gr-qc, hep-ph and physics to the broad count is not known. Whether the 18,660 base strictly excludes cross-lists has not been checked against arXiv's official monthly statistics.

**Several data issues remain open:**

- AstroMLab 5 concept tags were not examined.
- If they cover only January–July 2025, scaling to a full year raises seasonality questions.
- An all-years share may not represent 2025, given that eROSITA, Euclid and XRISM results were released that year.

**There is no year-on-year check.** No per-year cluster counts exist in the record. The 2024 figure is a non-independent rescaling.

**Borderline cases are unresolved.** It has not been decided how to count galaxy groups, cluster-scale simulations, and dark-matter or high-energy searches that use clusters only as targets.

**The run was budget-limited.** It used its single allotted wave.

## Open directions

1. Replace the assumed within-category fractions with a measured share by running the ADS abstract query for 2025 and hand-checking about 100 hits for star-cluster false positives.
2. Read Fig. 13 (and Fig. 12) of arXiv:2602.12303 with an OCR or pixel-reading tool, since any cluster sub-field bar divided by 18,660 would give a direct 2025 paper share.
3. Extract the galaxy-cluster concept share from the AstroMLab 5 tags (arXiv:2511.12353) for recent years as an independent anchor for the fraction.
4. Re-read the truncated passages of arXiv:2602.12303 to confirm the base and category counts verbatim and to check the definition of the 7,837 author-weighted contributions.
5. Obtain per-year cluster counts for 2023 and 2024 from the same query, so the 2025 figure can be checked against a real trend instead of the +14.2% total-paper growth.
6. Bound the gr-qc and hep-ph cross-list contribution to the broad count, using arXiv's per-category statistics or the API.
