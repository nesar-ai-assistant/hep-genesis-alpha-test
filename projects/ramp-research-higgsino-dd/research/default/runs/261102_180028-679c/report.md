## Abstract

A pure-higgsino thermal freeze-out was examined with the inelastic-DM server tools and a survey of recent arXiv literature. The perturbative tool (Kolb & Turner 5.26-5.29, fixed g*=86.25) gives Omega h^2 = 0.0731 at 900 GeV, 0.0898 at 1000 GeV, 0.1083 at 1100 GeV, 0.1181 at 1150 GeV, 0.1202 at 1160 GeV, 0.1285 at 1200 GeV and 0.1503 at 1300 GeV, interpolating to Omega h^2 = 0.120 at 1159 GeV (1150-1170 GeV band). This is offset from the canonical literature band 1.0-1.2 TeV, canonically 1.1 TeV, which includes Sommerfeld enhancement, coannihilation and bound-state effects not in the tool. Inelastic kinematics on xenon impose delta_max ~364 keV for v_max = 776 km/s; at 1100 GeV delta = 200 keV predicts ~5.1e4 events in 2.84 tonne-yr LZ exposure while delta = 350 keV predicts ~2 events, near the kinematic limit. A 900-1300 GeV x 100-650 keV scan finds 180 of 225 points pass relic+LZ+Fermi+CMB but none predict 0.3-5 LZ events, indicating tension with a single-event interpretation. Elastic Higgs-mediated scattering must be suppressed to <1e-48 cm2 to survive LZ 2024 and XENONnT, while Z-mediated inelastic scattering is ~7.4e-39 cm2. No quantitative comparison to Fermi-LAT dwarf/GC line limits or CTAO-North sensitivity could be completed.

## Background

The literature describes pure-higgsino thermal freeze-out with Sommerfeld and coannihilation corrections predicting Omega h^2 = 0.12 at m_chi ~1.0-1.2 TeV, canonically 1.1 TeV, with theoretical uncertainty from non-perturbative electroweak effects. Direct detection is dominated by Z-mediated inelastic scattering between nearly degenerate neutralinos with splitting delta ~100-400 keV, while Higgs-mediated elastic scattering is suppressed by high purity required by LZ 2024 and XENONnT limits. Recent work explores light-slepton coannihilation lowering the thermal mass to ~0.4-0.6 TeV and low-reheating cosmologies raising it, plus CTAO and Fermi-LAT line searches approaching the thermal annihilation cross section. The 2026 LZ 248 keV high-recoil event has renewed interest in inelastic higgsino benchmarks at 1.1 TeV and delta ~300-350 keV.

Key recent papers surveyed are arXiv:2609.01504 Higgsino Above the Sea of Fog, which derives LZ/XENONnT purity bounds and defines a benchmark delta ~350 keV near the neutrino fog; arXiv:2609.04175 The Higgsino Interpretation of the LZ Event with the High-Energy Sideband, which interprets the 248 keV event as inelastic scattering and constrains m_chi and delta from recoil energy and sideband; and arXiv:2609.33869 Heavy Higgsino Dark Matter With Low Reheating in View of the LZ High-Recoil Event, which shows low-reheating with T_rh <=595 GeV can accommodate >1.1 TeV masses. Canonical thermal-mass references include arXiv:2205.04486, arXiv:1411.6930, arXiv:1912.07795 and arXiv:1104.0228. Direct-detection limits are from LZ 2024 arXiv:2410.17036 and XENONnT arXiv:2303.14729, with the xenon neutrino fog from XLZD arXiv:2410.17137. Indirect limits are from Fermi-LAT dwarf work arXiv:1611.03184 and related line searches, and CTAO-North projections.

## Results

### Thermal relic density versus Planck

The server tool reports Planck Omega h^2 = 0.12 +/-0.0012 and computes perturbative freeze-out with x_f ~24.4-24.8, T_f ~36.3 GeV at 900 GeV to 53.2 GeV at 1300 GeV, and sigma_v scaling ~1/m_chi^2 as 3.73e-26 cm3/s at 900 GeV to 1.79e-26 cm3/s at 1300 GeV. The grid is Omega h^2 = 0.0731 (900 GeV), 0.0898 (1000 GeV), 0.1083 (1100 GeV), 0.1181 (1150 GeV), 0.1202 (1160 GeV), 0.1285 (1200 GeV), 0.1503 (1300 GeV). Linear interpolation between 1150 GeV and 1160 GeV gives a thermal mass of 1159 GeV where Omega h^2 = 0.120, with the 1160 GeV point itself at 0.1202 within 0.1 sigma of Planck. The tool has no Sommerfeld enhancement, no bound-state formation and no coannihilation delta-dependence, so this 1159 GeV value is perturbative only and not independently verified against the literature Sommerfeld+bound-state values.

### Annihilation cross section

For the thermal higgsino the tool gives sigma_v = 3.03e-26 cm3/s at 1.0 TeV (Omega h^2 0.0898, x_f 24.7, T_f 40.53 GeV), 2.50e-26 cm3/s at 1.1 TeV (0.1083, 24.6, 44.76 GeV), 2.29e-26 cm3/s at 1.15 TeV (0.1181, 24.5, 46.88 GeV) and 2.10e-26 cm3/s at 1.2 TeV (0.1285, 24.5, 48.98 GeV). Planck 0.12 is matched at ~1.15-1.16 TeV in this perturbative model. No quantitative 95% CL Fermi-LAT dwarf or Galactic Center line limits at 1.0-1.2 TeV and no CTAO-North projection numbers were extracted, so a comparison table versus the thermal cross section could not be completed.

### Inelastic kinematics and LZ 248 keV event

At m_chi = 1100 GeV the tool uses v_esc+v_E = 776 km/s and gives delta_max ~364 keV on xenon. Predicted kinematics are: delta = 200 keV peaks at E_R = 180 keV with v_min at peak 572 km/s and v_min at 248 keV = 579 km/s, accessible, predicting N = 51157.5 events in 2.84 tonne-yr with Poisson probability 1.0; delta = 350 keV peaks at 315.1 keV with v_min 757 km/s and v_min at 248 keV = 762 km/s, accessible, predicting N = 1.97 events with Poisson probability 0.86; delta = 500 keV peaks at 450.1 keV with v_min 905 km/s and v_min at 248 keV = 945 km/s, inaccessible, predicting N = 0.0. Elastic delta = 0 gives v_min at 248 keV = 336 km/s and N = 1.1e7 events if unsuppressed. The sharp drop from ~5e4 events at 200 keV to ~2 events at 350 keV reflects proximity to the kinematic threshold.

### Elastic versus inelastic cross sections

The tool auto-assigns Z-mediated inelastic sigma_n = 7.43e-39 cm2 at 1100 GeV (7.429930732553727e-39 cm2). Elastic Higgs-mediated scattering is quoted as suppressed to <1e-48 cm2 per arXiv:1912.07795, more than nine orders of magnitude below the Z-mediated value, which would otherwise give ~1.1e7 events. This suppression claim relies on a proxy mapping to high purity and small gaugino mixing and is not independently verified, and no numeric LZ 2024 arXiv:2410.17036 or XENONnT arXiv:2303.14729 SI limits at 1 TeV nor the XLZD arXiv:2410.17137 xenon neutrino fog value at 1 TeV were extracted to quantify decades below limits or position relative to the fog.
