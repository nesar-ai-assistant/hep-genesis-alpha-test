# Sterile-Neutrino Dark Matter and BBN Constraints

## Abstract

The surveyed literature separates into two physically distinct regimes. For keV sterile-neutrino dark matter, sufficiently long-lived states are generally not constrained primarily by decay-induced BBN effects; X-ray decay, relic abundance, and structure formation dominate. Minimal non-resonant Dodelson–Widrow production supplying all dark matter is excluded or strongly disfavored, whereas resonant production, mixed cold-plus-warm dark matter, and models with additional interactions retain conditional viable regions. For MeV–GeV heavy neutral leptons (HNLs), BBN excludes an intermediate lifetime and mixing regime through decay and energy-injection effects. The directly relevant 2024 analysis, arXiv:2410.07343, provides a two-parameter mass–flavor-mixing framework but does not establish a keV dark-matter window. Numerical boundaries for its electron-, muon-, and tau-mixing contours could not be recovered from the available record.

## Background

The keV sterile-neutrino literature identifies three broad production classes: non-resonant oscillation production, resonant Shi–Fuller production, and production through decays or interactions in an extended hidden sector (arXiv:1702.08430). Their predictions differ in abundance, momentum distribution, radiative lifetime, and free-streaming scale, so there is no production-independent mass–mixing relation.

In the minimal non-resonant scenario, the mixing required to produce all of the dark matter is incompatible with X-ray constraints and is also disfavored by small-scale-structure constraints. Non-standard neutrino self-interactions can shift or broaden the abundance-compatible mixing region, while a subdominant sterile fraction weakens the X-ray constraint (arXiv:2112.00758). A separate scalar-mediated freeze-in/freeze-out mechanism changes the relation between mixing, abundance, and momentum distribution; the hidden sector may thermalize at a temperature below the Standard Model temperature (arXiv:2204.08795).

Resonant production uses a large lepton asymmetry to modify the production history and generate a colder momentum distribution. The 7.1-keV mixed cold-plus-warm study in arXiv:2306.16532 evaluates the resulting structure suppression using Milky Way satellite counts rather than a complete BBN abundance fit. More recent work revisits resonant production with refined numerical treatment and identifies conditional keV-scale regions compatible with X-ray and Lyman-\(\alpha\) constraints for sufficiently large lepton asymmetry (arXiv:2510.01907). An Affleck–Dine mechanism with an \(L\)-ball provides another source of the required asymmetry, but the record does not give a numerical surviving mass–mixing interval (arXiv:2402.11902).

For heavier sterile states, BBN constraints arise from production, lifetime, temporary matter domination, and injection of mesons, photons, charged leptons, or neutrinos. Chen and Zhang analyze this regime using only the mass \(m_4\) and one flavor-specific mixing, \(\lvert U_{a4}\rvert^2\), for \(a=e,\mu,\tau\) (arXiv:2410.07343). Other 2024–2025 studies consider HNLs with axion-like-particle decay channels or dark decay modes, demonstrating that the BBN bounds are model-dependent (arXiv:2410.06970; arXiv:2507.12270).

## Results

### 1. Direct 2024 BBN analysis of heavy neutrinos

The directly relevant minimal analysis uses the two parameters
\[
(m_4,\lvert U_{a4}\rvert^2),
\]
with separate scans for electron, muon, and tau mixing. It includes the early-Universe production history, temporary matter domination, and injection from charged mesons, photons, and light neutrinos. The reported BBN-excluded region is complementary to laboratory HNL searches.

The qualitative exclusion is an intermediate-mixing window. At large mixing, the heavy neutrino decays before it can significantly affect BBN. At very small mixing, its freeze-in abundance is too small to appreciably change the light-element abundances. Very long-lived states are additionally subject to a separate CMB constraint.

The available evidence does not provide numerical contour coordinates for the electron-, muon-, or tau-mixing cases. Consequently, the result establishes the shape and physical origin of the excluded regime, but not a reproducible set of numerical mass–mixing boundaries.

### 2. Thermalized sub-GeV HNLs and dark decay modes

The 2025 analysis arXiv:2507.12270 reports that thermally equilibrated sub-GeV HNLs must have
\[
\tau_N \lesssim 0.02\ {\rm s}
\]
for successful BBN. Significant dark decay modes do not generically remove the cosmological constraint. Instead, they can strengthen it through additional radiation and changes to primordial helium and \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\).

This result concerns thermally equilibrated sub-GeV HNLs, not long-lived keV sterile-neutrino dark matter. It therefore supplies a lifetime benchmark for the heavy regime but does not define a viable keV dark-matter mass–mixing band.

### 3. Nonminimal HNL decay through an axion-like particle

The HNL-plus-axion-like-particle scenario of arXiv:2410.06970 reports a cosmologically consistent region approximately
\[
1\ {\rm MeV}\lesssim m_N\lesssim 1\ {\rm GeV},
\]
with active–sterile mixing strength approximately
\[
10^{-9}\lesssim \text{mixing strength}\lesssim 10^{-6}.
\]

In this scenario, HNLs decay primarily into an axion-like particle and a neutrino. The earlier HNL decay can relax ordinary BBN limits, although the subsequent axion-like-particle decay remains subject to cosmological and astrophysical constraints. This is a model-dependent MeV–GeV region rather than a standard keV sterile-dark-matter solution.

### 4. Minimal Dodelson–Widrow production

The self-interaction study arXiv:2112.00758 finds that minimal Dodelson–Widrow production supplying all of the dark matter is excluded by X-ray constraints in its benchmark analysis and is also disfavored by structure formation. Non-standard active-neutrino self-interactions can shift or widen the abundance-compatible mixing region toward either smaller or larger mixing.

Allowing the sterile state to be only a subdominant component further relaxes the X-ray constraint. The study reports no structure-formation problem from the additional interactions in its benchmark framework, but this conclusion depends on the assumed interaction model and production history.

No universal viable \((m_s,\sin^2 2\theta)\) interval follows from this result because the extra interaction parameters modify both production and the momentum distribution.

### 5. Resonant production and the required lepton asymmetry

Resonant Shi–Fuller production requires an initial lepton asymmetry many orders of magnitude larger than the baryon-to-photon ratio, while remaining below the CMB and BBN detectability limits quoted in the 7.1-keV study. The sterile-neutrino distribution is obtained from Boltzmann evolution, including a matter potential proportional to the lepton asymmetry.

The calculation in arXiv:2306.16532 assesses viability through satellite-host subhalo formation rather than a full primordial-abundance fit. Therefore, its structure-formation results should not be interpreted as a complete BBN-plus-X-ray exclusion analysis.

### 6. Mixed cold-plus-warm dark matter at 7.1 keV

For
\[
m_s=7.1\ {\rm keV},
\]
the mixed cold-plus-warm analysis retains models with at least 60 Milky Way satellite subhalos. It includes nearly all-sterile dark matter at an astrophysical mixing
\[
\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm astro})\simeq 7\times10^{-10},
\]
and reports viable values approximately below
\[
\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm astro})\lesssim 10^{-9}.
\]

The astrophysical and microscopic mixing angles are related by
\[
\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm astro})
=
f\,\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm tot}),
\]
where \(f\) is the sterile fraction of the dark matter. Thus, the quoted \(7\times10^{-10}\) and \(10^{-9}\) values are conditional on \(f\), the production history, and the adopted satellite-structure criterion. They are not universal microscopic mixing limits. The authors also state that updated scattering rates could lower the allowed maxima.

### 7. Scalar-mediated freeze-in and freeze-out

The mechanism in arXiv:2204.08795 produces keV sterile dark matter in two steps. A complex scalar sector is populated through Higgs-portal freeze-in, followed by sterile-neutrino production from scalar decays and annihilations. The hidden sector can thermalize at a temperature
\[
T_\phi<T,
\]
where \(T\) is the Standard Model temperature.

This changes the momentum distribution and free-streaming scale relative to oscillation production. The abundance, X-ray lifetime, and structure constraints therefore must be evaluated within the scalar benchmark; the mechanism cannot be represented by the minimal Dodelson–Widrow mass–mixing relation.

### 8. Refined 2025 resonant-production results

The refined resonant-production analysis arXiv:2510.01907 reports regions compatible with X-ray and Lyman-\(\alpha\) constraints for a lepton-to-entropy ratio
\[
L\gtrsim \mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
\]
and sterile-neutrino mass approximately
\[
m_s\gtrsim 20\ {\rm keV}.
\]

Production during lepton-number injection can shorten the free-streaming length and reduce the mixing required to obtain the observed abundance. These regions are conditional on the time-dependent lepton-asymmetry history and on the adopted X-ray and Lyman-\(\alpha\) analyses. They should therefore be read as viable benchmark regions, not as a model-independent mass–mixing band.

### 9. Affleck–Dine resonant production

The Affleck–Dine study arXiv:2402.11902 generates a large lepton asymmetry through an \(L\)-ball and uses it for Shi–Fuller resonant production. It revisits X-ray and Lyman-\(\alpha\) constraints, but the available abstract and evidence do not provide a numerical surviving interval in \(m_s\) and mixing.

The paper supports the existence of a motivated resonant-production mechanism, but no quantitative mass–mixing region can be reported from the available record.

### 10. Recent-literature coverage

The focused search identified arXiv:2410.07343 as the directly relevant minimal BBN analysis and found additional 2024–2025 papers on HNL dark decays, ALP cascades, and resonant keV production. No clearly relevant 2025–2026 paper combining standard keV sterile-neutrino dark matter with a dedicated BBN abundance analysis was returned by the recorded searches.

This is a search-record observation, not a proof that no such paper exists. The autonomous survey route failed with a search-interface runtime error, although direct full-text downloads succeeded for the principal papers. Full-text extraction and figure-caption verification were incomplete.

## Discussion

The central result is that “BBN-compatible” does not identify one common sterile-neutrino mass–mixing region. The keV and MeV–GeV regimes must be treated separately.

For long-lived keV sterile-neutrino dark matter, direct electromagnetic or hadronic decay injection during BBN is not the dominant issue in the cited literature. Viability is instead controlled by whether the production mechanism gives the observed relic abundance without violating the radiative-decay X-ray limit or erasing too much small-scale structure. Minimal Dodelson–Widrow all-dark-matter production fails this combined test in the cited benchmark studies. A subdominant sterile fraction, resonant production, additional neutrino interactions, or scalar-mediated production can reopen parameter space, but each option introduces additional parameters and correlated assumptions.

The most quantitative surviving keV benchmarks are conditional resonant-production results. At \(m_s=7.1\ {\rm keV}\), the mixed cold-plus-warm study permits \(\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm astro})\) near \(7\times10^{-10}\) for nearly all-sterile dark matter and values below approximately \(10^{-9}\) under its satellite criterion. The refined 2025 calculation instead identifies a broader mass-scale statement, \(m_s\gtrsim20\ {\rm keV}\), when \(L\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})\), with consistency between X-ray and Lyman-\(\alpha\) constraints. These are not directly interchangeable because they use different production histories, observables, and structure-formation assumptions.

The HNL BBN results are complementary rather than competing constraints on the keV dark-matter region. In the MeV–GeV regime, the 2024 analysis finds an intermediate-mixing exclusion caused by the interplay of abundance, lifetime, temporary matter domination, and decay products. The 2025 thermalized-HNL result gives the additional condition \(\tau_N\lesssim0.02\ {\rm s}\). The ALP-cascade result illustrates how new decay channels can alter this conclusion, leaving an approximate \(1\ {\rm MeV}\)–\(1\ {\rm GeV}\), \(10^{-9}\)–\(10^{-6}\) mixing-strength region subject to later ALP constraints.

Several attempted approaches did not produce stronger quantitative conclusions. The autonomous recent-literature survey failed because of a search-object runtime error. Direct downloads succeeded, but the remaining budget did not permit systematic extraction of all full-text passages, figures, captions, or exact contour coordinates. As a result, the numerical electron-, muon-, and tau-mixing boundaries in arXiv:2410.07343 remain unavailable, and the conditional keV regions cannot be converted into a single combined exclusion plot.

## Limitations

- The exact numerical BBN exclusion contours for \((m_4,\lvert U_{e4}\rvert^2)\), \((m_4,\lvert U_{\mu4}\rvert^2)\), and \((m_4,\lvert U_{\tau4}\rvert^2)\) were not extracted.
- The available record does not provide a complete joint scan combining BBN, relic abundance, X-ray flux, momentum-distribution moments, free-streaming, Lyman-\(\alpha\), and laboratory limits.
- The quoted \(7.1\)-keV and \(m_s\gtrsim20\)-keV resonant-production regions depend on different production assumptions and structure-formation criteria.
- The satellite count of at least 60 is a criterion in arXiv:2306.16532, not a universal structure-formation bound.
- The relation \(\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm astro})=f\sin^2(2\theta_{\rm tot})\) means that astrophysical mixing values cannot be interpreted as microscopic mixing values without specifying the sterile fraction.
- No numerical surviving mass–mixing interval was available for the Affleck–Dine scenario of arXiv:2402.11902.
- The survey was not exhaustive: one autonomous search path failed, and no claim of absence of all other 2025–2026 papers can be established.
- The report does not infer contour values from plots that were not quantitatively extracted; approximate numerical boundaries are therefore not supplied for the 2024 BBN scan.

## Open directions

1. Reproduce the electron-, muon-, and tau-mixing BBN contours of arXiv:2410.07343, including the temporary-matter-domination and decay-injection regions, and report numerical boundaries with the source assumptions.
2. Construct a single conditional keV scan combining relic abundance, radiative-decay X-rays, momentum distribution, free streaming, and a specified conservative Lyman-\(\alpha\) or satellite-structure baseline.
3. Reanalyze the resonant-production scenarios with explicit lepton-asymmetry histories and compare the \(m_s=7.1\ {\rm keV}\) mixed-component benchmark with the \(m_s\gtrsim20\ {\rm keV}\), \(L\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})\) region.
4. Extend the HNL comparison to dark-decay and ALP-cascade models, tracking the HNL lifetime, abundance, decay temperature, radiation density, and late-decay constraints.
5. Quantify how non-standard neutrino self-interactions and scalar freeze-in/freeze-out alter the abundance–mixing relation and the associated X-ray and structure bounds.
